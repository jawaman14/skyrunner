# The end-user polish plan (October 1986... er, 2026)

What the game needs to feel finished to someone who has never seen it, in the order the owner chose
(2026-10-04): the first hour, then the menus, then the world. Borrowed from open-world crime games (the phone,
the GPS line, markers, ground chases) and from Schedule I (dealers per district). Each step is its own PR.

## Done

- The phone is a handset with apps (Contacts, Messages, Map, Jobs, Bank, Radio, Settings); a call is a chat thread;
  offers buzz in as notifications with the keys that answer them.
- On foot and at the wheel: subtitles low in the middle, key caps for what you can do here.
- The round radar, the chart with its PLACES panel, click-to-waypoint, the pink beam, the dashboard arrow.
- The car's weight, its dashboard, one radio for the car and the cockpit.

## 1. The first hour

- **The title screen.** One screen with the logo over a slow flyover of the coast: Continue, New story, Open world,
  Multiplayer, Settings, Quit. The save's chapter and money on Continue.
- **An objective line.** Always one sentence saying what to do next, derived from the chapter's goals
  ("Take the 'Our own grass' job at Miller's Farm", "Fly the bales into a stash house"), with a waypoint and a marker
  on it. It follows the story: the next unmet goal, in the order the chapter lists them.
- **Mission markers in the world.** A glowing ring on the ground with a beam over it at the place the objective names:
  a job's pickup and drop, a buyer meet, the boss's desk, the car lot, a stash house. Walk or drive into it to act.
- **The opening.** The story's first minutes walk you through: the board, the load, the flight (with the
  approach the autopilot now flies), the car, the phone. Every step is a line and a marker, never a wall of text.

## 2. Getting around

- **A GPS line on the roads.** A waypoint set while on foot or driving draws a route along the roads (A* over the road
  graph) on the radar and the chart, with the next turn on the dashboard.
- **Wanted-level chases on the ground.** Police cars with sirens chase the car; roadblocks on the roads ahead; lose them
  out of sight and wait the stars down, as in the air.

## 3. The business

- **Dealers per district** (Schedule I): recruit a dealer you meet in a district; they sell your stock to that
  district's customers, need resupplying from a stash house, and take a cut. A district's demand and its heat.

## 4. The menus

- One modern look for every screen (jobs, load, hangar, desks, logistics, the casino, the dealership): the handset's
  palette and type, icons, the same key-cap footer, mouse everywhere.

## 5. The world (MAP_V2)

Kept exactly: every strip (position, heading, length, elevation), the HQs, the stash sites, the island. Rebuilt around
them, from nothing, with the new assets:
- the terrain (the natural pass is in: eroded hills, untouched strips and coasts);
- the city: avenues and streets (a street hierarchy, not a painted grid), a waterfront boulevard following the coast,
  a plaza at Club Tropicana with avenues off it, blocks of buildings that face their streets, parks;
- the roads: smooth curves, no overlap between the old planned roads and the town's streets;
- districts with their own look (downtown, the docks, the farms, the jungle, the scrub hills, the cove),
  walkable shops and interiors, props, vegetation by biome, lighting at night.
